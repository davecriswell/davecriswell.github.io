About

A New Englander with a calm temperament, who thinks first about the people on the other end of a system.

What I’ve always enjoyed most is watching how people actually use something, where they get stuck, and what they skip, and adjusting from there. That’s what drew me to software, and it’s still how I approach every system I build.

I coached high school and club soccer for about a decade, and it turned out to be similar work. You set people up to succeed, watch what actually happens, and adjust, patiently and without drama.

I’m also interested in where AI is genuinely useful in operations, and where it’s just noise. This site is part of that: I built it with Claude Code as a way to learn.

How I work

  1. 01

    Systems first, then the tool

    Understand how the work actually flows and who owns what, then choose software that fits. Not the other way around.

  2. 02

    Meet people where they already work

    Put the form in the app coaches already open. Adoption is the real test of a system, and the hardest one to pass.

  3. 03

    Show the math

    If a number can’t be traced back to its source, it isn’t ready to make decisions with.

  4. 04

    Start small, then scale

    Ship a manual fallback now, pilot with one team or region, learn what breaks, and earn the right to roll it out.

  5. 05

    Be honest about status

    “Automated through step four, manual after that” tells you more than “fully automated.” And sometimes step four is exactly the right place to stop.

  6. 06

    Write it down

    A process that lives in one person’s head isn’t a system yet.
